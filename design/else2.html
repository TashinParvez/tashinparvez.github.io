01 — Origin

How I got here

Dhaka, BD · 2026

It started with breaking things, not building them.

I didn't plan to become a PM. I was a CS student at UIU, deep in Codeforces problems at 2 AM, building systems that solved abstract puzzles. Somewhere around my 800th competitive programming problem, I had a quiet realization: I was more excited about why something should exist than about how the algorithm worked.

"The moment I stopped asking 'how does this compile' and started asking 'who actually needs this' — that's when product thinking clicked."

Teaching made me a better PM before I knew it.

As a Teaching Assistant across four courses, I spent semesters watching students struggle. Not with code — with understanding what the problem actually was. I was doing user research in a classroom. That instinct — diagnosing real friction, not assumed friction — is exactly what I do now as a Product Analyst at Ontik Technology.

What I'm building toward.

I want to work on B2B SaaS products where the users are professionals, the workflows are messy, and good product decisions directly move business metrics. I want to be in the room where trade-offs happen.

03 — Product Philosophy

Things I believe

These aren't clever quotes. They're principles shaped by real projects, real failures, and real trade-offs.

01

The best spec is the one engineers don't need to ask questions about.

Clarity in requirements saves more time than any productivity tool. I obsess over this because I've seen what vague specs cost — on both sides of the table.

02

Features are hypotheses, not commitments.

Every feature ships as a bet. The ones that work get doubled down. The ones that don't get cut. Ego has no place in a product roadmap.

03

Enterprise users deserve the same UX standards as consumers.

"It's B2B" is not an excuse for bad design. Professionals are humans too, and they notice when a product respects their time.

04

The PM who can't explain it simply doesn't understand it deeply enough.

Years of teaching taught me this. If your stakeholder update needs a glossary, the thinking isn't done yet.

05

Data tells you what. Conversations tell you why.

I use both. Analytics without user context is guessing with charts. User interviews without data is storytelling without evidence.
